# 1. Purpose

This document defines the canonical identifier strategy for the Phoenix Platform.

Its purpose is to establish a consistent, scalable, and technology-independent approach for identifying business entities across all domains, services, and future deployments.

The strategy ensures global uniqueness, interoperability, and long-term architectural stability.

---

# 2. Scope

This standard applies to:

- Logical Data Model
- Physical Database Design
- Business Entities
- Reference Entities
- Services
- APIs
- Integration Components
- Machine Learning Metadata
- Reporting

All entities requiring a persistent identity shall comply with this standard.

---

# 3. Design Principles

The identifier strategy is based on the following principles.

- Every business entity shall have exactly one canonical identifier.
- Identifiers shall be immutable.
- Identifiers shall have no business meaning.
- Identifiers shall remain stable throughout the entity lifecycle.
- Identifier generation shall not depend on a centralized service.
- The strategy shall support distributed and service-oriented architectures.

---

# 4. Canonical Identifier

The canonical identifier for all business entities is:

| Property | Value |
|----------|-------|
| Logical Type | Identifier |
| Physical Type | UUID |
| PostgreSQL Type | uuid |
| Nullable | No |
| Mutable | No |
| Unique | Yes |

Every entity shall expose a single primary identifier named `id`.

---

# 5. Business Identifier vs Technical Identifier

Phoenix distinguishes between technical identity and business identity.

| Identifier Type | Purpose | Mutable |
|-----------------|---------|---------|
| id | Internal canonical identity | No |
| external_code | External provider reference | Yes |
| business_code | Optional business identifier | Depends on business rules |

The `id` attribute shall never be exposed as a business code.

---

# 6. External Identifiers

Many entities originate from external systems such as:

- Stock Exchanges
- Brokers
- Market Data Providers
- Financial Information Providers

These identifiers shall be stored separately using `external_code` (or domain-specific attributes when multiple providers are supported).

External identifiers:

- are not primary keys;
- may change over time;
- are not guaranteed to be globally unique.

---

# 7. Identifier Generation

Identifiers shall be generated by the application layer or service responsible for creating the entity.

The database shall validate uniqueness but shall not be responsible for business identifier generation.

This approach supports:

- Microservices
- Offline processing
- Batch import
- Distributed deployments

---

# 8. Immutability

Once assigned, an identifier shall never change.

Operations such as:

- Merge
- Rename
- Reclassification
- Migration

shall not result in a new identifier unless a new business entity is created.

---

# 9. Uniqueness

The canonical identifier shall be globally unique.

No two entities, regardless of domain or service, shall share the same identifier.

---

# 10. Composite Keys

Composite primary keys are prohibited.

Business relationships may use composite unique constraints where required, but every entity shall still possess a single canonical `id`.

---

# 11. Surrogate and Natural Keys

Phoenix adopts surrogate keys as the canonical identity mechanism.

Natural keys (such as exchange symbol codes or ISO currency codes) may be enforced through unique constraints but shall not replace the canonical identifier.

---

# 12. Relationship Strategy

All logical relationships between entities shall reference the canonical `id`.

Business codes and external identifiers shall not be used as foreign keys unless explicitly required for integration purposes.

---

# 13. Identifier Lifecycle

The lifecycle of an identifier is:

```text
Generated
    ↓
Assigned
    ↓
Persisted
    ↓
Referenced
    ↓
Archived
```

An identifier is never recycled or reassigned.

---

# 14. Compliance

Compliance with this standard is mandatory.

All logical models, physical database designs, and application components shall conform to this identifier strategy.

Any exception shall require approval through the Architecture Governance process.
