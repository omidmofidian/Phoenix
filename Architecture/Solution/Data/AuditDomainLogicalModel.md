# 1. Purpose

This document defines the canonical Logical Data Model for the Audit Domain within the Phoenix Platform.

The Audit Domain provides enterprise-wide traceability by recording business events, security events, operational activities, and data changes occurring throughout the platform.

Its primary purpose is to preserve historical accountability, regulatory compliance, operational transparency, and enterprise governance without participating in business transaction processing.

The model is completely independent of database technology and implementation details and serves as the logical foundation for the physical audit architecture.

---

# 2. Scope

The Audit Domain defines the logical structure of enterprise audit information.

The scope includes:

- Audit Aggregates
- Audit ownership
- Audit relationships
- Business constraints
- Audit invariants
- Cross-domain references
- Logical lifecycle rules

The scope excludes:

- Business transaction processing
- Business entity ownership
- Operational workflows
- Database implementation
- Physical storage mechanisms
- Security implementation details

---

# 3. Architectural Role

The Audit Domain is classified as a **Supporting Domain** within the Phoenix Enterprise Architecture.

Its responsibility is to preserve immutable historical evidence of business activities generated by all operational domains.

The Audit Domain:

- records business events;
- records security events;
- records operational activities;
- records business state transitions;
- supports enterprise governance;
- supports regulatory compliance;
- supports operational traceability.

The Audit Domain never owns business entities and never participates in business decision making.

# 5. Aggregate Specifications

## 5.1 Audit Session Aggregate

### Business Purpose

The Audit Session Aggregate represents the logical execution context within which one or more audit events occur.

It provides end-to-end traceability for a business operation, system process, scheduled task, or user interaction.

### Business Responsibilities

- Establish an audit execution context.
- Group logically related audit events.
- Preserve execution chronology.
- Support enterprise traceability.
- Provide correlation across multiple business domains.

### Aggregate Root

Audit Session

### Business Identifier

Audit Session Identifier

### Logical Characteristics

- Aggregate Root
- Immutable after completion
- Append-only
- Historical entity
- Enterprise-wide scope

### Business Rules

- Every Audit Session shall have a unique business identifier.
- An Audit Session may contain multiple Audit Events.
- Audit Sessions are immutable once completed.
- An Audit Session shall preserve chronological ordering of its events.
- Audit Sessions shall never modify business data.

---

## 5.2 Audit Event Aggregate

### Business Purpose

The Audit Event Aggregate represents an immutable historical record describing a single business, operational, or security event occurring within the Phoenix Platform.

### Business Responsibilities

- Record business activities.
- Record security events.
- Record configuration changes.
- Record system events.
- Record integration events.
- Preserve historical accountability.
- Support governance and compliance.

### Aggregate Root

Audit Event

### Business Identifier

Audit Event Identifier

### Logical Characteristics

- Aggregate Root
- Immutable
- Append-only
- Historical entity
- Enterprise-wide visibility

### Business Rules

- Every Audit Event shall belong to exactly one Audit Session.
- Every Audit Event shall reference one originating Aggregate Root.
- Audit Events shall never be updated.
- Audit Events shall never be deleted.
- Event timestamps shall remain immutable.
- Historical integrity shall always be preserved.

# 6. Cross-Domain Relationships

The Audit Domain maintains logical references to business Aggregates throughout the enterprise while remaining independent of their ownership.

| Referenced Domain | Referenced Aggregates | Relationship |
|-------------------|----------------------|--------------|
| Reference Domain | Exchange, Market, Trading Board, Sector, Industry, Trading Calendar, Currency | Read-only reference |
| Core Domain | Company, Instrument | Read-only reference |
| Market Domain | Instrument Listing, Daily Market Data, Corporate Action | Read-only reference |
| Integration Domain | Data Provider, External Identifier | Read-only reference |
| Configuration Domain | Configuration Item | Read-only reference |
| Reporting Domain | Report Definition | Read-only reference |

The Audit Domain never owns, modifies, or controls the lifecycle of referenced Aggregates.

---

# 7. Domain Constraints

The following logical constraints govern the Audit Domain.

- Audit data shall be append-only.
- Audit records shall never modify business entities.
- Every Audit Event shall belong to one Audit Session.
- Every Audit Event shall reference only an Aggregate Root.
- Audit history shall remain complete throughout the lifecycle of the platform.
- Historical records shall never become invalid because of business entity changes.
- Audit information shall remain technology independent.

---

# 8. Domain Invariants

The following invariants shall always hold.

- Audit information is immutable.
- Audit Sessions preserve execution context.
- Audit Events preserve historical accountability.
- Referenced business Aggregates retain their ownership.
- Audit Aggregates never participate in business transactions.
- Historical consistency shall always be preserved.
- Business semantics remain independent of implementation technology.

# 12. Architectural Constraints

The following architectural constraints shall always be enforced.

- Audit Aggregates shall remain independent of business Aggregates.
- Audit Aggregates shall never own business entities.
- Audit Aggregates shall reference only Aggregate Roots.
- Audit data shall be immutable.
- Audit records shall be append-only.
- Historical integrity shall never be compromised.
- Business entity deletion shall never invalidate audit history.
- Audit information shall remain technology independent.
- The Audit Domain shall never participate in business transaction processing.
- All modifications to the Audit Domain architecture require architectural review.
